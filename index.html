Suppressing Hallucinatory Speech in Video-to-Audio Generation via Unlearning

Abstract Results Demo

Abstract

While end-to-end video-to-audio (V2A) generation has demonstrated promising results, the persistent occurrence of hallucinatory speech remains a key challenge for current V2A models. This phenomenon refers to the generation of audio that exhibits the acoustic properties of human speech while containing incorrect or nonsensical linguistic content. Given that speech content constitutes an extensive portion of the training corpus and explicit semantic modeling is absent, current V2A models are prone to generating hallucinatory, semantically-void speech. To address this challenge, we explore a machine unlearning framework in V2A generation. By separating speech into forgetting set and the remaining audio into retained set, our V2A model effectively mitigates hallucinatory speech generation and maintains the ability to generate other categories of audio. Besides, we propose a reliable metric to assess the frequency of hallucinatory speech, thereby validating the effectiveness of our unlearning framework. Experimental results show that our approach effectively suppresses hallucinatory speech while preserving the quality of generated Foley.
